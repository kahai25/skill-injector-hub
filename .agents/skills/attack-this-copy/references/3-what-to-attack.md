# 12 conversion killers to specifically probe

Ask the model about each of these by name if the general attack didn't surface them.

## 1. Value prop buried

Reader has to scroll or read 3+ paragraphs to learn what the product *does*. Hero should answer "what is this, for whom, and what changes" in <8 words + <20 word subhead.

## 2. Unexplained price

Price appears with no reason attached. "$49/mo" alone loses; "$49/mo — replaces $600 of tools" converts. Every price needs a comparison anchor.

## 3. Weak guarantee

"30-day money-back guarantee" that nobody believes because the refund process isn't linked, the terms aren't stated, and no customer testimony references it. Either commit to a real guarantee or drop it.

## 4. Competing CTAs

Hero has "Sign up", "Book a demo", "Watch video", "Read docs" — reader picks none. One primary CTA per section. Secondary actions get a text link, not a button.

## 5. Anonymous social proof

"Loved by 10,000 users" / testimonials from "Sarah M., Marketing Manager". No photo, no company, no LinkedIn — reads as fake. Use named humans with headshots or drop the section.

## 6. Feature list that lists features

Bullets like "AI-powered", "Real-time sync", "Enterprise-grade security" describe the product to itself. Bullets should describe outcomes for the reader — "Draft your proposal in 3 minutes instead of 3 hours."

## 7. FAQ answering fake objections

"Is this secure?" answered with "Yes, extremely." Real FAQs answer the objections that made someone almost not buy. "Why is this $X when [competitor] is free?" belongs on the page.

## 8. Screenshots without stakes

Product screenshots showing empty dashboards or "Chart Title" placeholder text. Screenshots should show *the moment of value* — a real result, a real name, a real number.

## 9. "AI-powered" without specifics

Any mention of AI without saying what it does, what model, and what the reader gets. "AI-powered scheduling" is noise; "Claude 3.5 reads your calendar and books the meeting" is a promise.

## 10. Founder photo missing on a small brand

Under $10M ARR, the reader wants to know a human is behind it. A founder photo + one-sentence "why I built this" beats every third-party logo wall.

## 11. Mobile hero that's just a logo + CTA

Desktop hero has the value prop; mobile hero got cropped to "Login" and a hamburger. Half of B2C traffic never sees the pitch. Design mobile-first.

## 12. Nowhere to say no

No "Not for you if…" section. Every good page tells some readers to leave — it makes the buyers trust the pitch. If everyone is your customer, no one converts.

## The universal test

Read the page out loud to a friend who has never heard of the product. If they can't tell you what it does, who it's for, and why they'd pay, none of the above matters yet — fix the value prop first, then attack the rest.
