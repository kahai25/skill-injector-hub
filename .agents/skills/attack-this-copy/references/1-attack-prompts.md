# Attack prompts by copy type

Copy-paste as-is. The wording matters — "attack", "list every reason", "quote the sentence" all shift the model out of encouragement mode.

## Landing page

> Attack this landing page. List every reason a visitor lands here and closes the tab without signing up. For each finding: quote the exact sentence or element, name the objection it triggers, and estimate how many visitors (out of 100) it loses. Do not suggest improvements. Do not compliment anything. Rank by severity.
>
> Specifically probe: is the value proposition clear in <5 seconds? Does the headline say what it *does* or what it *is*? Is the price justified before it's shown? Are there competing CTAs? Is social proof from named humans or anonymous "users"? Does the FAQ address real objections or fake ones?
>
> [Paste page text or URL]

## Pricing page

> Attack this pricing page. For each tier, list the reasons a qualified buyer picks the tier below it, or leaves entirely. Quote the specific line that triggers each doubt. Consider: is the middle tier obviously better than the cheap one? Is "Enterprise: contact us" a wall or a hook? Are the features named in customer language or product-internal jargon? Does the free tier make the paid tier feel like a rip-off, or a bargain?
>
> [Paste pricing text]

## Cold outreach / sales email

> Attack this email as if it landed in your inbox this morning between 87 other unread messages. When do you delete? Quote the exact word/phrase that makes you delete. Ignore politeness — I need to know what fails.
>
> Specifically: is the subject line curiosity-driven or "COMPANY | Category | Q3 Outreach"? First line about them or about us? Ask small enough to say yes in 3 seconds? Any word that appears in every sales email ever (leverage, synergy, quick chat, hop on a call, circle back)?
>
> [Paste email]

## Sales proposal / SOW

> Attack this proposal as the buyer's CFO who has never met us and doesn't want to spend the money. List every question they'd ask that this proposal doesn't answer. Quote the exact place where a claim is unsupported. Flag every place a price appears without a reason attached. Do not defend anything.
>
> [Paste proposal]

## App Store / Play Store description

> Attack this app store listing. A user is scrolling the category page, sees this listing, and taps back within 3 seconds. Why? Quote what killed it — the icon, the first screenshot, the subtitle, the first line of description. Ignore the walls of text below the fold; on mobile store pages, users almost never read them.
>
> [Paste listing text or screenshots]

## Product Hunt / launch post

> Attack this launch post. You're a jaded PH hunter who upvotes maybe 2 of 40 daily launches. What makes you scroll past? Quote the sentence. Flag every "AI-powered" / "revolutionary" / "reimagining" that reads as slop. Flag any claim without a specific number or a specific comparison.
>
> [Paste post]

## The universal follow-up

After the model returns findings, if it starts hedging or complimenting:

> You're being polite. I asked for an attack. Redo it. Every finding must quote a specific line and name a specific reader who bounces. If you can't attack something, say "nothing to attack" — do not fill space with praise.
