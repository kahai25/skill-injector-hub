# Competition — red ocean, blue ocean, category creation

From *Blue Ocean Strategy* (Kim & Mauborgne, 2005). Overused but the core idea is genuinely useful.

## Red ocean

A market with defined competitors, defined customers, defined product shape. You compete on price, features, and marketing spend. Margins bleed.

- Ride sharing (Uber vs Lyft vs Bolt)
- Web hosting (AWS vs GCP vs Azure vs Cloudflare)
- Note-taking (Notion vs Obsidian vs Evernote vs Apple Notes)

You can win in a red ocean, but only with a real moat (see moats reference). Otherwise you're just fighting for scraps.

## Blue ocean

A market where the competitive rules are undefined because the category is new or you've reframed it. No direct competitors, so no price war.

- Cirque du Soleil — reframed circus as adult theater, escaped the kids-circus red ocean.
- Airbnb — created "peer-to-peer lodging" instead of competing with hotels head-on.
- Notion (early) — reframed docs+wiki+db as one tool.

## Category creation

The strongest blue-ocean play: you name a new category and become the default. Salesforce = CRM/cloud. HubSpot = inbound marketing. Datadog = observability. Rippling = employee-graph HRIS.

Rules for creating a category:

- **Name it before anyone else does.** "Inbound marketing" was HubSpot's, then everyone used it.
- **Educate more than you sell.** Founders of new categories run podcasts, write books, host summits — because they have to teach the buyer why this category exists.
- **Anchor to a shift.** "Remote-first HR", "AI-native CRM", "post-cookie attribution". A new category needs a why-now.
- **Be #1 for 3+ years or die.** Second place in a category you created is worthless — the winner takes the vocabulary.

## When to fight in a red ocean anyway

- You have a moat competitors don't (Stripe entering a crowded payments market with dev-first UX).
- You can subsidize (Amazon entering everything).
- The incumbents are asleep (Slack vs enterprise chat, 2013).

## When to run

- No moat, no category-creation angle, no cost advantage, and the incumbents are awake. Pick a different problem.
