# Moats — the five real ones

A moat is a structural reason a competitor can't just copy you. Only five exist. Everything else ("we execute better", "our design is nicer", "our founder is 10x") is a temporary advantage, not a moat.

## 1. Network effects

The product gets more valuable to each user as more users join. Direct (WhatsApp — need people to message), two-sided (Airbnb — hosts and guests), data (Waze — more drivers = better traffic), local (Uber — network per city).

**Test:** if you removed 50% of users, does the product get materially worse for the rest? If yes, network effects.

## 2. Switching costs

Leaving is painful. Not just annoying — actually costly in time, data, or risk.

- **Data lock-in** — QuickBooks: 10 years of books in a proprietary format.
- **Workflow lock-in** — Salesforce: sales team retrained, integrations rebuilt.
- **Contract lock-in** — enterprise multi-year deals.
- **Trust lock-in** — Stripe: works flawlessly, and payments is a "don't break it" category.

**Test:** would a customer switch for a 20% price cut? If not, switching costs are real.

## 3. Scale economies

Bigger you get, lower your unit cost, so you can price under any new entrant. Amazon logistics, Costco purchasing, Google's ad-auction scale.

**Test:** is your cost-per-customer-served actually dropping as you grow? Most SaaS thinks they have scale economies and don't — cloud costs are basically linear.

## 4. Brand

A category-defining brand people trust by default. Not "we have nice branding" — "Kleenex", "Xerox", "Google it". Takes decades and billions. Rare and slow to build.

**Test:** do customers say your brand name when they mean the category? If not, you have marketing, not brand-as-moat.

## 5. Proprietary technology / IP

Genuine technical difficulty a well-funded team can't replicate in 12 months. Rare in software. Common in biotech, semiconductors, aerospace.

**Test:** could a top-tier team clone the core tech in a year with $10M? If yes, no tech moat.

## Fake moats

- **"First-mover advantage"** — usually a myth. Google wasn't the first search engine. Facebook wasn't the first social network.
- **"Better UX"** — copyable in a quarter.
- **"AI"** — if you're just wrapping an API, so is your competitor.
- **"Community"** — communities move. See every dying subreddit.
- **"Founder-market fit"** — a great story for investors; not a moat.

## What to actually do

Early on, you probably have no moat. That's fine. Build **toward** a moat: design the product so that every user adds value for the next (network effect), or so switching is painful (data + workflow lock-in). If none of the five is available in your category, you're in a service business or a red-ocean commodity — price accordingly.
