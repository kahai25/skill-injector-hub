# SaaS shapes — vertical, horizontal, platform, marketplace, aggregator

Five common business shapes for a modern software company. Pick one deliberately. Trying to be more than one at the start is how you die.

## Vertical SaaS

Software for one specific industry. Deep domain features, priced high, sold slowly, sticky forever.

- Toast — restaurants
- Procore — construction
- Veeva — pharma
- ServiceTitan — HVAC / plumbing

Pros: less competition, higher ACVs, deep moats via workflow integration and industry expertise. Cons: smaller TAM, need a founder who knows the industry, sales cycles are long, has to survive industry cycles.

## Horizontal SaaS

Software for one job across all industries.

- Slack — communication
- Notion — docs
- HubSpot — marketing
- Zendesk — support

Pros: huge TAM, PLG-friendly, viral growth possible. Cons: crowded, price-compressed, generic — often outcompeted by vertical players who go deeper.

## Platform

Software that other companies build on. You provide primitives; they provide applications.

- AWS, Stripe, Twilio, Shopify (platform side)

Pros: revenue scales with the ecosystem, moat compounds with third-party integrations. Cons: takes years and $100M+ before ecosystem matters. Do not choose this on day one.

## Marketplace

You don't own supply or demand — you match them and take a cut.

- Airbnb, Etsy, Fiverr, Upwork

Pros: capital-light, network effects. Cons: chicken-and-egg is brutal; unit economics only work at scale; regulatory risk (Uber, Airbnb).

## Aggregator (per Ben Thompson)

You control the demand side and force suppliers to compete for it. The definitional shape of modern internet giants.

- Google — aggregates users, publishers compete for ranking.
- Facebook — aggregates attention, publishers/advertisers compete.
- Amazon (retail) — aggregates shoppers, sellers compete.

Pros: crushing power once dominant. Cons: requires zero-marginal-cost acquisition and near-infinite supply. Almost impossible to build a new one from scratch.

## How to choose

Rules of thumb:

- Founder has 10+ years in an industry → **vertical**.
- Founder has one narrow product idea used by many kinds of teams → **horizontal**.
- Founder has a devtool other companies will integrate → **platform** (but ship a real product first, ecosystem later).
- Founder wants to match two sides → **marketplace**, and you'd better have a plan for the chicken-and-egg.
- Founder thinks they'll be the next Google → **do something else** unless you have a genuinely new distribution mechanism.
