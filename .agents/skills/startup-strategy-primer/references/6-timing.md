# Market timing

Bill Gross (Idealab) analyzed 200 startups. The #1 predictor of success was **timing** — above team, idea, business model, or funding. Too early is worse than too late: too early means no one wants it; too late means someone else already won.

## Signs you're too early

- Every conversation starts with explaining the category. "Wait, what does 'observability' mean?"
- Buyers say "cool" but have no budget line for it.
- The infrastructure to serve customers cheaply doesn't exist yet.
- Adjacent enabling tech (mobile, cloud, LLMs, cheap bandwidth) is 2-5 years out.

Examples: Webvan (2000, right idea, wrong decade — Instacart won in 2012 once smartphones existed). Segway. VR before 2020. Every "voice assistant for X" from 2016-2019.

## Signs you're too late

- Three incumbents each have >$100M ARR in your exact space.
- Category has a name everyone uses (see category creation).
- Customers have already bought once, have a vendor, are mid-contract.
- Investors' first response is "isn't this crowded?"

Sometimes late is fine — if you have a real 10x differentiator (Superhuman entering "crowded" email in 2017) or a new distribution channel (TikTok-first brands entering CPG post-2020).

## Signs the timing is right

- The category is being talked about but no one has won.
- Customers ask you if you've heard of the (small, poorly funded) other companies in the space.
- Enabling technology just hit — LLMs 2023, WebRTC ~2015, cloud ~2008, mobile ~2010.
- Regulatory change forces buyers to move (privacy laws, sustainability reporting).
- A macro shift makes the old way expensive or embarrassing (remote work + Zoom, 2020).

## How to use timing

- Ask "why now?" until the answer is specific. "Because AI got cheap" is not enough — "because GPT-4o hit $2.50/M input tokens making per-user AI unit economics work under $10/month subscriptions" is.
- If you're 2+ years too early, either build something simpler that works today, or wait. Do not raise a Series A on a market that doesn't exist yet.
- If you're late, find an underserved vertical inside the category — that's a beachhead move (see beachhead reference).
