# Beachhead markets

A beachhead is a narrow, specific first market that you can dominate before expanding. Named after Normandy — you don't invade all of France at once, you take a beach.

## Why narrow beats broad

- **Concentrated demand** — a small community that all shares one pain will tell each other.
- **Focused product** — solving one job perfectly beats solving ten jobs okay.
- **Word of mouth** — dentists talk to dentists; SaaS founders talk to SaaS founders.
- **Fewer competitors** — huge markets attract giants; niches don't.

## Canonical examples

- **Amazon** — books only for 4 years. Predictable SKUs, no perishables, mail-friendly. Then adjacent: CDs → DVDs → electronics → everything.
- **Facebook** — Harvard undergrads only. Then Ivy League. Then colleges. Then everyone.
- **Tesla** — $109k Roadster for tech-rich early adopters, then Model S (luxury), then Model 3 (mass market).
- **Uber** — SF black cars for tech workers, then UberX, then everywhere.
- **Stripe** — YC startups. Every YC founder needed payments; each one told the next batch.

## How to pick a beachhead

Five filters, all must be true:

1. **You can reach them cheaply.** A community that already gathers online (subreddit, Discord, industry Slack, LinkedIn group).
2. **They share one pain acutely.** Not "would be nice", "must have this Monday".
3. **They have money and can decide.** Not a $50/mo product for a Fortune 500 procurement dept.
4. **You'll learn things that generalize.** Solving payments for YC startups taught Stripe things useful for Shopify merchants later.
5. **The market is big enough to be a real business but small enough that no one else bothers.** The sweet spot.

## Anti-pattern

"We're building for everyone" = building for no one. Every big company started with a beachhead. Every failed startup thought they were the exception.
