# Market sizing — TAM / SAM / SOM

Three nested circles. Investors want the number; the number matters much less than showing you understand the nesting.

- **TAM (Total Addressable Market)** — total annual revenue if literally every possible buyer on Earth bought your thing. Ceiling. "Global CRM spend is $80B/yr."
- **SAM (Serviceable Addressable Market)** — the slice you could realistically reach given language, geography, regulation, distribution. "English-speaking SMBs in NA + EU with 5-100 employees = $8B/yr."
- **SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market)** — what you can actually win in the next 3-5 years given your team, product, GTM. Usually 1-5% of SAM. "0.5% of SAM in 5 years = $40M ARR."

## Bottom-up beats top-down

Top-down: "The market is $80B, we just need 1%." Investors hate this — it's not a plan. Bottom-up: "There are 200k dental practices in the US, we can reach 30k through the ADA channel, average contract $6k = $180M SAM." Investors love this — you've done the work.

## Worked example — an AI onboarding tool for B2B SaaS

- **TAM:** global B2B SaaS spend on onboarding tooling ≈ $4B (rough Gartner-style estimate).
- **SAM:** English-speaking B2B SaaS with >100 customers and dedicated CS teams ≈ 50k companies × $8k/yr ≈ $400M.
- **SOM:** 3% of SAM in 4 years via Product Hunt + LinkedIn + partner channel = $12M ARR.

## When TAM matters

- Fundraising — VCs need $1B+ TAM for the math to work on a fund-returning outcome.
- Prioritizing features — you build for SOM, not TAM.
- Deciding to pivot — if SAM is <$50M, you have a lifestyle business, not a VC-backable one.

## When TAM doesn't matter

- Bootstrappers — a $10M SOM you fully own is better than a $100M SOM where you have 2%.
- Early product decisions — talk to 20 SOM customers first.
- Pricing — costs and willingness-to-pay drive price, not TAM.
